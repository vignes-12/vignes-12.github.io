Research Interests

I build physics-informed generative AI that is judged by how faithfully it models the physical world, starting with spectroscopic single-molecule localization microscopy (sSMLM).

Mainstream AI evaluates diffusion models and U-Nets on benchmark scores such as F1 or FID, which say little about whether an output obeys physics; physical and biomedical imaging, in turn, have been slow to adopt modern generative AI. My work sits in that gap. I pair Monte Carlo simulators and digital twins of real optical systems with denoising and diffusion models, then measure success in physical terms: localization precision, spectral accuracy, robustness at low signal-to-noise, and transfer from simulation to experimental data. In teaching, I hope to bring applied machine learning to students through project-based courses built on real scientific data.

Projects

A connected line of work on physics-based simulation and deep learning denoisers for spectroscopic single-molecule localization microscopy (sSMLM), each project building on the last.

DWP-sSMLM (tentative): dual-wedge-prism digital twin for single-channel spectral imaging

Aug 2026 – Present
  • Investigating whether the non-linear, wavelength-dependent dispersion of a dual wedge prism (DWP) lets one dispersed image recover both molecular position and emission spectrum, removing the photon-splitting spatial channel of conventional sSMLM.
  • Building a Monte Carlo forward model with calibrated DWP dispersion and a wavelength-dependent PSF; validating identifiability with joint maximum-likelihood estimation, Cramér–Rao bounds and ambiguity maps against a linear-disperser control.
  • Developing a full-frame DWP simulation pipeline (crop, classify, denoise) that models experimental sSMLM data more faithfully than grating-based simulators. Exploratory, early-stage project.

FastSpecDiffuse (FSD): warm-start initialization for accelerated diffusion inference

Jul 2026 – Present
  • Initializes SpecDiffuse sampling from an SA_SpecUNet (or alternative) estimate instead of pure noise, outperforming naive DDPM timestep respacing.
  • Cut sampling from 5–10 to 2–3 timesteps with no loss in denoising performance on simulated and experimental data.
  • Ongoing: comparing initializers and characterizing the mechanism by which warm starts improve inference.

SpecDiffuse: robust conditional denoising diffusion model for sSMLM

Jan 2025 – Present
  • Built a lightweight conditional diffusion (DDPM) model on an SA_SpecUNet backbone, extending the published EMDiffuse framework to sSMLM; built from scratch in Python/PyTorch.
  • Trained on DWP-based physics simulations; transfers to experimental data and performs especially well on low-SNR datasets.
  • Improved resolution ~50% over prior U-Net implementations on low-SNR datasets.

SA_SpecUNet: spatial-attention residual U-Net with multi-scale background simulation

Jan 2025 – Present
  • Built a multi-scale Perlin-noise background simulator for more realistic training data.
  • Added spatial attention gates inspired by Attention U-Net (Oktay et al., 2018) and reframed the task as residual prediction rather than direct background prediction.
  • Faster and more accurate than comparison methods, improving resolution ~25% over SpecUNet.

SpecUNet: supervised deep learning framework for single-molecule spectroscopic image denoising [Paper]

Sep 2024 – Jul 2025
  • Developed grating-based Monte Carlo simulations (100k+ images) to generate ground truth for sSMLM data.
  • Trained a U-Net (built in MATLAB, then ported to Python/PyTorch for configurability and scalability) to predict background and denoise spectral images, improving resolution ~70% over conventional methods.
  • Published in Analytical Chemistry (2025, cover article) and Proc. SPIE (2026).

SIMBA: agentic AI platform for single-molecule multidimensional imaging [Website]

Aug 2025 – Present
  • Led the denoising module: packaged the lab's denoisers as Python packages and built their integration into the SIMBA application.
  • Integrated the published SpecUNet; SA_SpecUNet, SpecDiffuse and FastSpecDiffuse are planned next.
  • SIMBA is a multi-agent desktop application (Electron, React, GPT-4o, LiteLLM) whose LLM agents interpret user intent and orchestrate localization, spectral analysis and denoising tools.
